D1.3) Explanation
Please explain in a clear and comprehensive manner why the award of the contract without
prior publication in the Official Journal of the European Union is lawful, by stating the
relevant facts and, as appropriate, the conclusions of law in accordance with the directive:
In the context of the ERC project Rural Riches, an integrated analysis of ancient DNA and
isotopes is planned on the human skeletal remains from the early medieval cemetery of
Lent (the Netherlands). The cemetery consists of 50 inhumation graves and 20 cremation
graves. The graves all date to the sixth century CE. The focus of the research is on the
social dynamics of a local community. Next to this research, a detailed study of physical
anthropological aspects, the abundant grave goods, the grave structures, and the date of the
graves will be carried out
Specific requirements for the biomoleculair part of this project concern:
• Genetic sexing
• Kinship analysis
• Genetic ancestry
• Population affinity
• Isotopic provenancing (Sr and O isotopes)
• Isotopic palaeodietary reconstruction (C and N isotopes)

aDNA analysis must be carried out with genome wide capture hybridization kits with the
additional option of shotgun sequencing, targeting both the nuclear and mitochondrial
genome. The produced genetic data should be compatible with previously published data on
early medieval populations such as Amorim et al. 2018, O’Sullivan et al 2018 and Veeramah
et al 2018.
With regards to the isotope analyses, the strontium isotope column extractions must be
carried out in an ISO certified clean laboratory following an extraction protocol that
requires a minimal amount of destructive sampling (max. 2 mg of enamel power). A similar
prerequisite applies to the other isotopic systems: the applied protocols must require a
minimal amount of destructive sampling (maximum 1 mg for oxygen and maximum 30 mg
for carbon and nitrogen isotopes).
It is a prerequisite that the aDNA and isotope research form a truly integrated research
project. We therefore do not aim at two independent research efforts. The party involved
must show that 1) they have a track record in aDNA and archaeological isotope research and
the integration of both research lines, 2) are applying state of the art methodologies, 3) that
they have experience in analysing (early medieval) human remains from the Netherlands and
other parts of northwest Europe, 4) and are participating in multidisciplinary archaeological
teams.
