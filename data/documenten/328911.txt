2. Procedure
2.1 Procedure
Title: Voluntary transparency: Service to isolate Sporozites (Malaria parasites) with a Free 
Flow Electrophoresis device
Description:
The contracting authority, Leiden University Medical Centre (LUMC) has the intention to 
acquire services of the University of New South Whales to isolate sporozoites (malarial 
parasites) with a recently purchased Free Flow Electrophoresis device . LUMC has 
established that no other competitors than the University of New South Whales are, 
momentarily, able to meet some of the essential technical demands with respect to proven 
technology. 1
Procedure 
identifier: ab514467-b9ab-4659-acbb-ba3174211936
Internal 
identifier: L-EU-24-04
Type of 
procedure: Negotiated without prior call for competition
The procedure 
is accelerated: No
Main features 
of the 
procedure:
UNSW is in a unique and irreplaceable position to implement this task. It is the sole 
global expert in a technology involving dissection-independent isolation of viable, 
infectious malaria parasite sporozoites, a technology originally developed by Professor 
Jake Baum at Imperial College London (https://www.life-science-alliance.org/content/4/7
/e202101094). The technology (for which Professor Baum holds the Patent, which has 
since been assigned to UNSW) has been further developed at UNSW towards GMP 
compliance making it uniquely suitable for the EU CAPTIVATE program. Whilst there 
are reports of unrelated methods that claim to deliver in vitro derived sporozoites without 
requiring mosquitoes from labs in the US (methods that have not been replicated outside 
of these labs), the technology at UNSW is – to the best of our knowledge – the only 
method available that can deliver sporozoites without mosquito dissection, can be adapted 
to any malaria-infected mosquito, i.e. is agnostic to parasite strain and is developable to 
be compliant with EU GMP standards for human use. The core technology (an FFE 
machine) and capacity to train are in place at UNSW (our in-kind contribution to the 
program), with costs requested used for consumables to test each step and experimentally 
validate cryopreservation methodologies plus associated FTE salary that will additionally 
ensure transfer of the methodology (and deliver training) to LUMC. Given that the Baum 
lab is the only centre of know-how for this technology, which would require substantial 
additional costs to re-establish or mimic elsewhere (which may not even be possible) and 
given there is no alternative technology that is agnostic to parasite strain (e.g. can be 
applied to the parasites proposed in the EU CAPTIVATE program) we believe it would 
be fruitless and entirely unreasonable to tender for an alternative process/provider. To 
reiterate, another group/service provider would incur substantial additional costs to 
replicate our workflow unreasonably delaying the program with no guarantee of success. 
By means of this call for voluntary ex ante transparency, LUMC informs parties of its 
intention and provides the opportunity to submit a reasoned objection against this 
intention. If parties have objections against the intended purchase from UNSW, please 
inform LUMC on the last appearance of April 2nd , before 17:00 h. by sending an email 
directed at: aanbestedingen@lumc.nl with reference to Jimmy Been, Procurement. When 
during this term no objections have been raised, LUMC is free to acquire it’s desired 
scope from the mentioned firm
